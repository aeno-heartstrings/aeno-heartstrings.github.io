Heartstrings

The internet used to be a place where you met people. Then it became a place where you rated them.

Heartstrings is a connection platform. We are building the half of the category nobody has fixed: not who you meet, but what happens after.

What exists

For thirteen months Heartstrings ran as a live product inside Discord. People arrived through game communities, passed a live voice verification with a human before they could use anything, and were introduced to each other by a matching engine rather than handed a list to scroll.

It ran in production for thirteen months without going down. It was built and operated by one person, with no funding, no paid acquisition, and no marketing budget of any kind. Every user, every match and every dollar was earned.

How we handle our own numbers

Every figure above is reconciled against raw payment and database exports, row by row. Where a number has been wrong, the correction is published with the old value, the new value, and the direction it ran.

That includes the corrections that were flattering us. We revised our own lifetime payer count down from 219 to 73, and the share of users who ever matched down from 97.4% to 68.5%, before anyone asked. A ledger that only ever flatters is not a ledger.

Where this is going

The Discord year proved the connection layer and the trust layer. It did not prove the game layer, and that is the open bet. Heartstrings Connect is the standalone platform: verification, introductions and a library of small co-operative games built to be played by two people who have just met.

What kills every subscription product in this category is that a matching service gives you no reason to come back once it works. A library does. That is the thesis, and it is a thesis rather than a result.

The round
