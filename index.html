01 What this is

Heartstrings is a connection company. The dating industry made the introduction efficient and left what happens after it untouched, which is where most connections die.

We build co-operative games that two people play together, and the company earns from the game library rather than from keeping anyone single.

02 What exists

Heartstrings has run as a live platform for thirteen months. It is in production today, on Discord, operated by one person. Every profile on it passed a thirty-second live voice call with a human before it was approved. It was built and operated with no funding and no paid acquisition.

There is no company entity yet and no standalone app yet. The figures below come from the platform that is running.

04 How we handle our own numbers

Corrections run in whichever direction the data goes.

Every figure this company publishes carries its denominator and its as-of date. When one turns out to be wrong it is corrected in public, with the old value printed beside the new one and the reason attached. Three that ran against us:

Was published

97.4% of users ever matched

Now stands at

68.5%, being 318 of the 464 who liked someone

What happened

The old figure divided 598 lifetime identifiers by the 614 current profiles, when 1,962 accounts had passed the gate across the platform's life. 273 of those identifiers belonged to people who had already departed. Overstated by 45 points.

Was published

25.1% all-time patron churn

Now stands at

74.0%, being 54 of the 73 who ever paid

What happened

The old denominator counted every roster row, including 146 followers who never pledged and therefore could never churn. This is the company's headline monetisation risk and it is three times worse than first reported.

Was published

Roughly 7,000 views on our own writing

Now stands at

149 lifetime views, on the one article whose statistics page has been read

What happened

Either the claim describes a different and older piece of writing or it is wrong. It is logged as contradicted and held out of use until that is settled, rather than quietly softened.

One more, printed because a reader would otherwise do the subtraction. 1,962 people passed the voice gate across the platform's life and 614 hold a profile today, so at least 1,348 have left. That is a survival ceiling of 31.3% and a departure floor of 68.7%.

It carries no time dimension, so it is not a churn rate. A departing user's row is deleted, so departure erases its own evidence and the true figure can only be worse than the bound, never better.

05 Where this is going

Stated as a bet, because that is what it is.

A library of co-operative games sits in front of the connection layer, released in seasons. The wager is that an experience worth sharing acquires people below dating-app cost, where another profile to swipe does not, and that a library gives a reason to renew that a matching service structurally cannot give. Revenue then comes from the games rather than from anyone staying single, so a user who finds somebody is a customer who keeps paying instead of a subscription that ends.

What is not known, said before it is asked. Paid acquisition has never been run here, so the cost of acquiring a user is unmeasured rather than modelled. The automated verification that replaces the voice call has no activation rate yet. Nothing in the record above shows whether a match worked, because the Discord schema logs no match outcome at all. Those three are what the money buys first.

The company takes credit for the environment and never for the outcome. Nothing here claims that a match caused a relationship.
